Overleaf's free trial: how long it lasts, what it unlocks, and how to cancel

Yes, Overleaf has a free trial. It lasts 7 days, gives "full access to your chosen plan", and is offered on the Student, Standard and Pro plans. Your card is charged when it ends unless you cancel first. (Overleaf: plans and pricing, as of November 2026)

Written by the Aldine project, so read it with that in mind. Statements about other products are taken from the linked pages of that product's own documentation. Checked on 24 November 2026.

The trial in one table

Everything here comes from Overleaf's own pages as they read on 24 November 2026. Overleaf's plans page is authoritative if anything has changed since.

QuestionWhat Overleaf says
Is there a free trial of premium?Yes. The plans page FAQ has an entry headed "How does the free trial work?"
How long?7 days: "You get full access to your chosen plan during your 7-day free trial".
Which plans?Student, Standard and Pro each carry a "Try for free instead" link. So do the Standard and Pro cards on the group tab. Organizations shows "Contact Sales", and the free plan has nothing to trial.
Is a payment method needed?Overleaf's pages do not say directly. The FAQ says "Your card will be charged at the end of your trial unless you cancel before then", and individual plans are paid "online via credit card, debit card, or PayPal". No Overleaf page describes a trial without one.
What unlocks?Everything in the plan you picked ("full access"). What each plan includes is on the plans page and in Overleaf's premium features docs.
What happens at the end?The card is charged and the subscription continues, unless you cancelled. There is "no obligation to continue beyond the trial."
How to cancelAccount, then Subscription, while logged in. The trial keeps running "for the full 7 days" after you cancel, and you are not charged.

For what the free plan itself includes, and what the paid plans cost, see Is Overleaf free? What the free plan includes and what is paid.

Cancelling during the trial

You can cancel "at any time via the subscription management page", and "If you do this before the end of the seven-day trial period, you won't be charged anything, but the trial will remain active for the full period." (Overleaf docs: canceling your subscription, as of November 2026)

The steps it gives:

  1. On your project dashboard, click the Account icon in the lower left, then Subscription.
  2. Click "Stop Your Subscription".

So cancelling on day one costs you nothing in trial time. You still get the full week.

What happens to your projects afterwards

Nothing is deleted. The plans page FAQ says "Changing or canceling your plan won't affect your projects, the only change will be to the features available to you." (Overleaf: plans and pricing, as of November 2026)

Collaborators are the part to plan for. When an account moves to the free plan, Overleaf's premium features page says you keep every project you own but are "allowed only one collaborator on new and existing projects". If you invited several co-authors during the trial, its advice is to choose who keeps edit access and move the rest to view-only before you downgrade; otherwise all of them become view-only and you pick one again afterwards. Track changes already made stay in the project, though you can't add new ones. (Overleaf docs: premium features, as of November 2026)

Upgrading instead of trialling

If you already know you want a paid plan, you can buy it directly from the same cards ("Get Student", "Get Standard", "Get Pro"). Overleaf's renewal page explains how a change mid-term works: upgrading, or moving to an annual plan, "will take effect immediately", with the price "pro-rated with the remaining time on your current subscription". Other changes wait until the end of the billing period. Most subscriptions renew automatically. (Overleaf docs: renewing your subscription, as of November 2026) (Overleaf: plans and pricing, as of November 2026)

The student plan

Student is a paid plan at a discount, and it has the same 7-day trial as the others. Overleaf describes it as "Available to students currently enrolled at a degree-granting institution", and its FAQ adds that the discount "is only for students at educational institutions. This includes graduate students." (Overleaf: plans and pricing, as of November 2026)

Against the free plan, the main differences Overleaf lists are 10 collaborators per project instead of 1, and track changes. (Overleaf docs: premium features, as of November 2026) The full comparison is on our free plan page.

Overleaf Commons: premium through your university

Before paying as a student or researcher, check this one. Overleaf Commons "allows universities and organizations to provide access to Overleaf premium features across an entire campus or company, or to specific groups." (Overleaf docs: intro to Overleaf Commons, as of November 2026)

How you join, per Overleaf's user docs:

  1. See whether your institution is listed on Overleaf's universities page. A listed institution links to its own Overleaf portal page, which says who is eligible.
  2. In Account Settings, under Email and Affiliations, click "Add new email" and enter your institutional address.
  3. Confirm it. Depending on the institution that is either a link in a confirmation email or signing in through your institution's single sign-on with "Link Accounts".
  4. If you are eligible, your Subscription page then says you have a Professional Overleaf account.

(Overleaf docs: Commons subscription, as of November 2026)

You don't need a new account for this; your existing one can join. Two points from the same docs. AI access under Commons is "at the Basic allowance level" and "The AI assistant is not included." And membership has to be renewed: users "must periodically reconfirm their institutional affiliation", usually every 18 months, and anyone who doesn't, or who leaves, goes back to the free plan without losing their projects. (Overleaf docs: Commons subscription, as of November 2026) (Overleaf docs: user unenrollment, as of November 2026)

If your institution has no Commons subscription, Overleaf's admin docs point to a sales contact for the institution, not for individuals. (Overleaf docs: intro to Overleaf Commons, as of November 2026)

Premium features in someone else's project

One more route that Overleaf documents itself. Some premium features belong to the project, not to you: "track changes, compile time, and access to full history" are "based on the project owner's subscription", so a free-plan user invited to a subscriber's project can use them there. Integrations such as Dropbox, Zotero and GitHub linking stay with the subscriber's account. (Overleaf docs: premium features, as of November 2026) So when the owner of a paper's project has a subscription, co-authors on the free plan get those project-level features in that project.

A different model: Aldine

Aldine is an open-source, self-hosted collaborative LaTeX editor under AGPL-3.0. There is no trial because there are no plans. Every feature is in the one free edition and the number of collaborators is not limited; you provide the server. The install is two containers with Docker Compose, described in the self-hosting guide.

That is also the catch. There is no hosted Aldine service today (one is not ruled out), so someone in your group has to run it. Aldine is young: first release July 2026, 1.0 in October 2026. Review works through comments with suggested edits, not keystroke-level track changes. The full list of limits is in the self-hosting guide.

When Overleaf's trial is the better choice

  • You want to test premium features on a real paper this week and have no server. The trial needs only an Overleaf account, and you can cancel on day one without losing days.
  • Your university has Overleaf Commons. Then you may not need a trial at all; check your institutional email first.
  • You need track changes. Overleaf has them on the Student, Standard and Pro plans. (Overleaf docs: premium features, as of November 2026) Aldine has review comments with suggested edits instead.
  • Your co-authors are already on Overleaf and nobody wants to move a project mid-revision.

Common questions

Does Overleaf have a free trial?

Yes. Overleaf's plans page describes a 7-day free trial with full access to the plan you choose. It is offered on the Student, Standard and Pro plans. (Overleaf: plans and pricing, as of November 2026)

How long is the Overleaf free trial?

Seven days. If you cancel during the trial, Overleaf says the trial still runs for the full seven days and you are not charged. (Overleaf docs: canceling your subscription, as of November 2026)

Can I try Overleaf premium without a credit card?

Overleaf's pages do not describe a trial without a payment method. The FAQ says your card is charged at the end of the trial unless you cancel, and individual subscriptions are paid online by credit card, debit card or PayPal. The free plan needs no payment at all. (Overleaf: plans and pricing, as of November 2026)

Is Overleaf free for university students?

The free plan is open to everyone, students included. Premium features cost money unless your university has an Overleaf Commons subscription, in which case you join it by adding and confirming your institutional email address in your Overleaf account settings. Otherwise there is a discounted Student plan for students enrolled at a degree-granting institution. (Overleaf docs: Commons subscription, as of November 2026)

Can I use Overleaf premium features through my institution or a co-author?

Overleaf documents two routes. Your institution may have an Overleaf Commons subscription that you can join with your institutional email. And some premium features, such as track changes, full history and the longer compile time, follow the project owner's subscription, so they work for free-plan collaborators inside a subscriber's project. (Overleaf docs: premium features, as of November 2026)

What happens to my projects when the trial ends?

If you cancel, you keep your projects. Overleaf says changing or cancelling a plan only changes the features available to you. Back on the free plan you keep every project you own, but only one collaborator can have edit access per project. (Overleaf docs: premium features, as of November 2026)
