Papeeria alternatives: what Papeeria offers and other options for collaborative LaTeX

Papeeria is a hosted LaTeX and Markdown editor with real-time collaboration, git sync and a free plan. This page is for readers who need something Papeeria is not built as, such as a self-hosted or open-source editor. Here is what Papeeria offers according to its own site, how to take a project with you, and which other editors cover those cases.

Written by the Aldine project, so read it with that in mind. Statements about other products are taken from the linked pages of that product's own documentation. Checked on 24 November 2026.

What Papeeria is

Papeeria runs in the browser and calls itself "LaTeX and Markdown online". You can "Use pure LaTeX or mix it with Markdown." A full TeX Live environment is preinstalled, so a browser is all you need. (Papeeria home page, as of November 2026)

The editor has real-time collaboration, discussions in the margin, autocompletion, a spellchecker and a math preview. Plots can be built with gnuplot or Plot.ly. On sharing, the home page says "No collaborator count limits!", and on syncing: "Keep your work synced with your Git repository, Google Drive or Dropbox folder." There is a mobile version too. (Papeeria home page, as of November 2026)

Two plans are listed. Epsilon is "Free forever" and includes unlimited public projects and "One active private project" (archived projects do not count). Delta "Starts from $5/month" and raises that to "Up to 10 active private projects", with more quota available to buy. Git sync on Epsilon gives "Full read-write access to public repositories"; on Delta it covers private repositories as well. File edit history goes back 24 hours on Epsilon and 30 days on Delta. (Papeeria pricing, as of November 2026) Prices change; Papeeria's pricing page is authoritative.

Neither page carries a notice about a change of service, and sign-up is open.

Why people look at other editors

The reasons below are about deployment and workflow, not about the quality of the editor.

  • The files have to stay on your own server. Some universities and labs require that. We found no self-hosted edition on Papeeria's site or pricing page.
  • You want the source code. An open-source editor can be audited, patched and run without an account with any vendor.
  • Git is the centre of your workflow. If your group works with branches and pull requests, you may want branches inside the editor, not only a sync to one repository.
  • A different pricing shape fits. Papeeria prices by active private projects. Other editors price by collaborators, or cost nothing beyond the server you run them on.

Taking a project with you

Papeeria documents two ways out. The home page, under "No lock in", says: "You can download projects as ZIP archives at any moment and work offline with your local TeX distro." (Papeeria home page, as of November 2026) The other is git sync, which keeps the project in a git repository you control; on the free plan that works with public repositories, on Delta with private ones too. (Papeeria pricing, as of November 2026)

Before you move, check one thing by hand: Markdown. Papeeria lets you mix Markdown into LaTeX, and an editor that expects plain LaTeX may not build those files the same way. Typeset the project locally or in the new editor and compare the PDF with Papeeria's.

The options

Overleaf

Overleaf is the closest like-for-like move if you want to stay hosted. A Papeeria ZIP goes in through New project, then "Existing project (.zip)". The upload takes up to 180 files, single files of at most 50 MB, and no more than 7 MB of editable material; larger projects go in as a subset first. (Overleaf docs: uploading a project, as of November 2026)

Its plans are counted differently from Papeeria's. Overleaf's free plan lists "1 collaborator per project"; Student and Standard list 10 and Pro unlimited, and the git, GitHub and Dropbox integrations are on the paid plans. (Overleaf plans, as of November 2026) Prices change; Overleaf's plans page is authoritative. Overleaf can also be self-hosted as Community Edition or Server Pro; what each includes is on self-hosting Overleaf.

Aldine

Aldine is our project: an open-source (AGPL-3.0) collaborative LaTeX editor that you run yourself, in two Docker containers. It imports a project from a ZIP file. That import is tested with Overleaf and generic ZIPs, not with Papeeria exports in particular, so typeset once and look at the result.

  • Real-time co-editing with live cursors, and no limit on collaborators.
  • Each project is a real git repository. You create branches and merge them in the browser, and push and pull to GitHub, GitLab, Gitea or Forgejo. More on that in git with Overleaf and Aldine.
  • Review comments with suggested edits, and Zotero sync for a library, group library or single collection.
  • Nothing is behind a paid tier.

What it does not have: a hosted service (someone on your side runs it), keystroke-level track changes, and Markdown support: its README lists none. It is also young, with a first release in July 2026 and 1.0 in October 2026. The self-hosting guide lists every limit.

When Papeeria is the better choice

  • You do not want to run a server. Papeeria is hosted; Aldine is not.
  • You write in Markdown and LaTeX together. Papeeria supports mixing the two. (Papeeria home page, as of November 2026)
  • Your team is large but your private papers are few. The free plan lists no collaborator count limit, with one active private project. (Papeeria pricing, as of November 2026)
  • You keep papers in Dropbox or Google Drive, or want gnuplot integration. Both are listed for Papeeria's Delta plan. (Papeeria pricing, as of November 2026)

Common questions

Is Papeeria free?

It has a free plan, Epsilon, listed as "Free forever", with one active private project and unlimited public projects. The paid Delta plan "Starts from $5/month". Prices change; Papeeria's pricing page is authoritative. (Papeeria pricing, as of November 2026)

Papeeria or Overleaf?

Both are hosted LaTeX editors with free and paid plans, and the plans are cut differently. Papeeria's free plan limits private projects but not collaborators; Overleaf's free plan lists 1 collaborator per project. Compare the two plans pages against your own team size and number of private papers. (Papeeria pricing and Overleaf plans, as of November 2026)

How do I move a project from Papeeria to Overleaf?

Download the project from Papeeria as a ZIP, then upload it in Overleaf with New project and Existing project (.zip). Overleaf's upload takes up to 180 files and at most 7 MB of editable material. (Overleaf docs: uploading a project, as of November 2026)

Can I self-host Papeeria?

We found no self-hosted edition on Papeeria's site or pricing page. If you need the editor on your own server, look at Overleaf Community Edition or Server Pro, or at Aldine.
