# The Aldine name

The AGPL covers Aldine's *code*. It does not cover its *name*. "Aldine" and the
Aldine logo are marks of Tobias Rahloff, and this page says how you may use
them. The point is narrow: nobody should be able to stand up a service called
Aldine that isn't this one, because then "Aldine" stops telling anybody
anything.

Everything below is permission you already have. None of it restricts what you
may do with the source.

## Yes, without asking

- **Run it.** Host Aldine for yourself, your lab, your department, your company,
  your customers. Call it Aldine. Charge for it if you like.
- **Redistribute it unmodified.** Packages, images, mirrors: keep the name.
- **Say what your thing works with.** "Deploys Aldine", "an Aldine plugin",
  "imports from Aldine", "faster than Aldine". Comparisons and criticism too.
- **Write about it.** Articles, talks, courses, videos, screenshots, tutorials.
- **Fork it.** Of course. See the naming note below.

## Ask first

- **A modified version distributed under the Aldine name.** Patches that change
  behaviour and then travel as "Aldine" make bug reports meaningless. Name it
  something of your own and say "based on Aldine" instead.
- **A hosted service whose name or domain is built on "Aldine"**, for example
  "Aldine Cloud", "Aldine Hosting", or `aldine-*.com`. Run the software as a
  service freely: the AGPL grants you that and this page does not take it back.
  Just give the service your own name.
- **Logo alterations**, or using the mark in a way that suggests this project
  endorses or maintains your product.

## Not allowed

Registering "Aldine" or a confusingly similar name as a trademark, company name,
or domain for LaTeX, publishing, or collaborative-editing software.

## In practice

If your users could reasonably think they are getting the thing maintained at
[github.com/trahloff/Aldine](https://github.com/trahloff/Aldine), use the name.
If they would be wrong about that, use your own.

Uncertain? Open an issue and ask. The answer is usually yes, and the answer is
never a lawyer's letter as the opening move.
